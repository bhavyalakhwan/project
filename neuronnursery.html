Week 1 · Neuron Nursery

The Neuron

The basic building block of everything you think, feel, and do. 86 billion of them are firing inside your skull right now.

What is a Neuron?

A neuron is a specialised cell in the nervous system whose entire job is communication. Unlike most cells in your body, neurons are built to send and receive messages — electrical and chemical signals that travel at incredible speeds.

Your brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons, and they never work alone. Every thought you have is the result of millions of neurons firing together in coordinated patterns.

The Three Main Parts

Every neuron has the same core structure — three parts that work together to receive, process, and send information.

🌿

Dendrites

Branch-like extensions that receive incoming signals from other neurons.

🔵

Cell Body

The core of the neuron. Processes all incoming signals and keeps the cell alive.

⚡

Axon

A long tail that carries the electrical signal away toward the next neuron.

How Does it Fire?

When a neuron receives enough signals through its dendrites, it generates an electrical impulse called an action potential. This charge travels down the axon at speeds of up to 120 metres per second.

At the end of the axon, the electrical signal is converted into a chemical message — neurotransmitters are released into the gap between neurons, called the synapse. The next neuron picks them up and the signal continues. This is how a single thought travels across your entire brain in milliseconds.

"A single neuron can form up to 10,000 connections with other neurons — your brain has an estimated 100 trillion synaptic connections total."
— NeuroGarden · Week 1 Fact
🤖

The AI Connection

Artificial neural networks — the technology behind modern AI like ChatGPT — were directly modelled after biological neurons. Each artificial "neuron" receives numerical inputs, processes them, and passes a signal forward, just like the real thing. The deeper you understand biological neurons, the better you'll understand why AI works the way it does.
